00

System thesis

One-line schematic

BDash is not thirteen tools sharing a sidebar. It is one claim, repeated in thirteen places: every piece of paper, call and job in a small field business should land in a single ledger, and that ledger should tell you within one glance whether the month is survivable. Everything downstream — the buckets, the collections chase, the margin audit — is that claim being enforced.

01

Operating rules

Two invariants, applied everywhere

Most small-business software is a filing cabinet with charts. BDash's design difference is that it holds two rules and refuses to let any module contradict them. Read the rest of the blueprint through these.

RULE 01

Cash flow stays net positive

Money in must exceed money out, measured monthly, shown on the first screen. Nothing else in the system is allowed to look healthy while this is red.

Consequence: growth decisions — ad spend, hiring, the custom build itself — draw from margin, never from reserve or debt.

RULE 02

Every bucket clears its threshold

Spending is divided into seven named buckets, each with a minimum monthly funding requirement. A bucket below its line is flagged before month close, not discovered at tax time.

Consequence: pricing is judged against buckets. A service that can't cover its share of the allocation gets repriced or dropped — it doesn't get to hide inside a good month.

Why this is the strongest idea in the system

A tank you can read from across the room converts an accounting abstraction into a physical fact: the Emergency tank is short, everything else is above the line. Six of seven met is a sentence an owner acts on before dispatch. A profit-and-loss statement is not.

02

Module specifications

13 modules · purpose · features · use case · verdict

Each module below is documented the same way: what it is for, what it actually contains, the concrete moment it earns its place, what it depends on, and a candid verdict. Verdicts are marked Proven pattern (this works in real small businesses today), Needs review (sound idea, requires a professional or a hard technical decision), or Scope risk (this is an entire product category on its own).

OP-01

Overview

Proven pattern
Purpose

The forty-second screen. Answers one question before the trucks leave: is this month survivable, and what is the single most expensive thing being ignored right now?

Features
  • Cash strip — in, out, docs stored, open threads, unpaid invoices, month to date
  • Threshold tanks — seven buckets, funded level against a dashed requirement line
  • Trailing six-month cash chart — in versus out, so a bad month reads as a trend or a blip
  • Attention feed — items pulled from every module and ranked, collections first
  • Weekly 20-minute review — a fixed Monday checklist, the system's one required habit
  • Build gate tracker — counts consecutive net-positive months toward permission to build
Use case
The 24th · 6:52 AM · before dispatchEmergency is $310 short with six days left, and $1,180 is sitting in two invoices past thirty days. The owner doesn't open a report to learn this. He taps Chase now, makes two phone calls at 9:00, and the shortfall closes from money already earned rather than from reserve.
Depends on
OP-05 FinanceRC-01 InventoryPR-02 PayrollOP-04 Digi-Scan
Verdict Strongest module. It resists the usual dashboard failure — a wall of metrics with no verb attached — because every row ends in an action or a flag. The one weakness is ranking: "collections first" is hardcoded reasoning that should eventually be a rule the owner can see and change.
OP-02

Day Log

Proven pattern
Purpose

Runs the working day and then seals it. Today is a plan; yesterday is a closed file with its paperwork attached.

Features
  • Task list with times — dispatch, collections calls, quote visits, restock, end-of-day scanning
  • Tap to complete; unfinished work rolls forward at close-out rather than vanishing
  • Daily notepad — the same entry the Journal owns, edited from either screen
  • Close-out — bundles tasks, journal and the day's scans into one dated archive folder
  • Yesterday panel — completion count, docs archived, journal excerpt, cash position at close
Use case
Friday · 6:10 PM · tailgate downSix of seven tasks done, van oil change untouched again. Close-out rolls it to next week, seals eleven scanned documents into the day's folder, and stamps the day's net at +$610. Nothing is remembered; everything is recorded.
Depends on
OP-03 JournalOP-04 Digi-ScanRC-01 QC blocks dispatch
Verdict The sealed day file is the quiet innovation here — it makes "what happened on August 1st" a single retrievable object instead of a search across five systems. Rolling unfinished tasks forward needs a limit, or the third-rolled task becomes permanent furniture.
OP-03

Journal

Proven pattern
Purpose

Institutional memory for a business that otherwise keeps its history in one person's head. Conversations, commitments and decisions, dated.

Features
  • Autosaving entry for the current workday, synced with the Day Log notepad
  • Archive list — every previous day, newest first, with a one-line preview
  • Reader pane — full entry as written, plus copy-into-today for carrying a thread forward
  • Plain-text export of the whole archive
  • Seeded sample days so an empty archive still demonstrates its own shape
Use case
Six weeks later · a disputed invoiceA client insists no payment date was ever agreed. The July 30 entry says otherwise: Alvarez committed to paying the $730 by the 8th. Not a legal instrument, but a contemporaneous record that changes how the conversation goes.
Depends on
OP-02 Day LogPR-01 Clients
Verdict In the prototype this is device-local storage with an in-memory fallback — fine for a demo, fatal in production. Entries about clients and money belong in the same record store as everything else, attached to the client file, backed up with it.
OP-04

Digi-Scan

Needs review
Purpose

The intake mouth of the whole system. Paper goes in; a searchable, transcribed, triple-stored record comes out. Every other module's data quality is decided here.

Features
  • Drop or scan — invoices, receipts, contracts, HR forms, anything on paper
  • Four-stage pipeline — scan, transcribe, PDF, write to Drive and database, shown per document
  • Low-confidence gate — uncertain totals block storage until a human clears them
  • Triple store — cloud drive, API database, local mirror, written in one pass
  • Checkpoint restore and archive verification
Use case
17:30 · fuel receipts on the dashEleven documents photographed in four minutes. Ten flow straight through and auto-tag as deductibles. One invoice returns a shaky total and refuses to file itself until someone looks — which is the point. A wrong number that enters the ledger silently is worse than a document that never filed.
Depends on
OP-05 FinanceRC-02 FilesPR-02 HR docs
Verdict Conceptually the best-defended module — human-in-the-loop on low confidence is the correct answer and most products skip it. The open questions are engineering ones: which OCR service, what confidence threshold, what happens when the drive sync and the database disagree, and how the local mirror is encrypted on a truck laptop.
OP-05

Finance

Needs review
Purpose

The ledger and the two levers that move it fastest: getting paid sooner, and charging correctly.

Features
  • Six-line ledger — loans, taxes set aside, deductibles, reserves, savings, funds
  • Statements — cash flow, expense by bucket, quarterly P&L, year-to-date deductibles package
  • Collections — automatic reminders at 7, 14 and 30 days, deposits on new work, tap-to-pay on site
  • Price & margin audit — average job against true cost from the bucket model, with a verdict per line
  • Auto-tagged deductibles pulled from scan transcriptions
Use case
Month close · the margin auditHedge trim bills at $60 and costs $58. The business has been busy doing it for a year and quietly paying for the privilege. The audit reprices it to $85 and the change syncs to the website quote form the same afternoon — the fix reaches customers without a second task.
Depends on
OP-04 Digi-ScanSL-01 PricingPR-01 Clients
Verdict Collections and repricing are the highest-return, lowest-cost features in the entire blueprint, and correctly placed in phase one. Tax set-aside and deductible classification, however, are accountant territory — the module should present the numbers and export cleanly rather than imply it has made a tax determination.
SL-01

Services & Pricing

Proven pattern
Purpose

One price book, with margin attached to every line, and a disciplined place to put the exceptions.

Features
  • Catalog by category — maintenance, cleanup, trim, add-ons — with current price and live margin
  • Repricing state shown inline as old price → new price
  • MISC register — custom and special prices with the reason and the approver recorded
  • Floor check — a custom price must still clear its bucket allocation to be saved
  • Sync to the website quote form on change
Use case
A twelve-property HOA bidBook rate is $240; the volume bid needs $205. Instead of a handshake, the price is logged with the reason, the approver, and a floor check confirming 27% margin. Two years later nobody has to reconstruct why that account is cheaper than the others.
Depends on
OP-05 Cost modelSL-02 Quote formPR-01 Clients
Verdict The MISC register is the underrated feature — undocumented discounts are how small service businesses bleed without noticing. Requiring a reason and an approver on every exception is a governance control disguised as a form field.
SL-02

Website & Ads

Needs review
Purpose

Demand generation with a closed loop: traffic must arrive somewhere that converts, and finished jobs must produce reviews that lower the cost of the next lead.

Features
  • Traffic panel — weekly visits and leads from the business site
  • Campaign manager — create, schedule and monitor ads with spend, leads and cost per lead
  • Review engine — job marked complete triggers a review request text two hours later
  • Online booking and instant quotes — form to mailbox to auto-response, measured in seconds
  • Deposit at booking — 50% upfront on new work, feeding collections
  • Ad spend charges the [Operationals] bucket automatically
Use case
Nineteen quote requests, one monthEach gets an auto-response inside 45 seconds promising a call before 5 PM. Eleven convert to booked jobs with deposits collected. The reviews from those jobs move the rating from 12 reviews to 47 — and the cost per lead in the panel above falls without raising ad spend a dollar.
Depends on
PR-03 MailboxSL-01 PricingOP-05 Deposits
Verdict The review engine is close to free and compounds — correctly identified as the cheapest campaign available. The ad management side is the weakest fit: rebuilding campaign tooling that the ad platforms already provide is effort spent away from the system's actual advantage. Read the numbers in, leave the buying where it lives.
PR-01

Clients

Proven pattern
Purpose

A relationship record, not a contact list. It stores the things that keep an account, which are rarely the things in the invoice.

Features
  • VIP cards — private notes, preferred service day, communication style, active custom rate
  • One-tap call, text, and full history per client
  • Regulars table — cadence, last touch, balance and days outstanding
  • Unified client file — invoices, calls, texts, emails, contracts and notes in one view
Use case
A two-year account, worth three referralsThe note says: prefers Friday service, ask about the dog. The crew lead who has never met her asks about Biscuit. That is the entire retention strategy, and it survives staff turnover only because it was written down.
Depends on
PR-03 Comms trailOP-05 BalancesRC-02 Documents
Verdict Right instinct, and cheap to build. Note that "notes are private to you" is a promise about access control — once a crew lead can open a client file, the personal notes need a permission boundary or the promise quietly becomes false.
PR-02

Payroll & HR

Scope risk
Purpose

Handles a workforce that is never all one thing: employees, subcontractors, temp-to-hire, and crews borrowed from other companies.

Features
  • Mixed roster — in-house, sub/contract, temp-to-hire with week counters, partner transfers
  • Payroll run and HR document export
  • Partner exchange board — rent, trade or swap crews and trucks under contract
  • Contract generation — every transfer produces a PDF routed through attorney review and Digi-Scan
  • Mandatory legal gate on classification and labor-law review before any transfer executes
Use case
Peak season, no permanent hireRiverside Lawn Co. offers two crew and a box truck for two weeks. Capacity doubles for a fortnight with no payroll commitment afterward — provided the contract survives attorney review, which the board treats as a blocking step rather than a formality.
Depends on
OP-04 Digi-ScanOP-05 Payroll bucket05 Risk register
Verdict The partner exchange is a genuinely original idea — elastic capacity without hiring, which is the hardest problem in seasonal field work. It is also the highest-liability feature in the blueprint. Worker classification and crew-lending arrangements carry IRS and labor-law exposure that no dashboard can absorb. Build the board as a contract tracker; do not build a payroll tax engine.
PR-03

Mailbox & Phone

Needs review
Purpose

Puts every client conversation — email, call, text — inside the same system that holds their invoices, and rescues the leads a working crew inevitably misses.

Features
  • In-house mailbox — business email threads alongside the record they concern
  • Phone and text box — business-line SMS and call log
  • Missed-call auto text-back within 60 seconds
  • Voicemail and call transcription, attached to the client file automatically
  • Web-form auto-response with a stated callback time
Use case
11:02 AM · nobody can answer, everyone is on a mowerThe call goes unanswered. Forty-one seconds later the caller has a text offering a quote. Seven leads recovered this month — roughly $1,680 of work that would otherwise have gone to whoever picked up second.
Depends on
PR-01 ClientsSL-02 Lead capture
Verdict Missed-call text-back is the highest return per dollar in the whole document and available off the shelf this week. Building an in-house mail client, by contrast, is a decade-old solved problem — sync the threads, don't rebuild the inbox. Call recording also carries consent requirements that vary by state and need checking before it ships.
RC-01

Inventory & Quality Check

Proven pattern
Purpose

Protects billable hours. A missing spool of trimmer line and a mower that won't start cost the same thing: a day.

Features
  • Stock counts against reorder points, with below-line items flagged to the Overview feed
  • Reorder list builder and count logging
  • Weekly equipment QC — mowers, trimmers, blower, van, card reader
  • Overdue detection — a check not logged becomes a flag on its own
  • Dispatch block — failed or overdue equipment cannot be assigned to tomorrow's jobs
Use case
Saturday flag, Monday savedTrimmer line reads 2 against a reorder point of 5. The restock lands on Saturday's task list, not on Monday morning at a customer's gate with three properties still to cut.
Depends on
OP-02 Day LogOP-05 Equipment bucketOP-01 Attention feed
Verdict Small module, disproportionate value, and the dispatch block is the right kind of enforcement — the system refuses an unsafe assignment rather than warning about it. Watch for override fatigue: if the block can be dismissed with one tap, it becomes decoration within a month.
RC-02

Files & Exports

Proven pattern
Purpose

The retrieval side of Digi-Scan. Filing is only worth doing if finding is fast, and if the whole set can leave the building for an accountant or an auditor.

Features
  • File cabinet — invoices, contracts, HR records, tax and deductibles, receipts, with volume indicators
  • Auto-tagging of tax documents from transcription
  • Client relations view — paper and communication trail per account
  • Packaged export — HR and finance bundles for outside professionals
Use case
Tax season · one email instead of one weekendThe accountant asks for the year. The year-to-date deductibles package is already compiled from documents that were tagged the day they were photographed. The shoebox never existed.
Depends on
OP-04 Digi-ScanOP-05 FinancePR-01 Clients
Verdict Sound, and the export path is what keeps the system honest — data that can leave cleanly is data the owner still owns. Search is the missing feature: a file cabinet with counts but no full-text search across transcriptions wastes the transcription step.
TA-01

Recovery Plan

Proven pattern
Purpose

The module that governs the other twelve. It sequences the turnaround and holds the permission slip for building anything custom.

Features
  • Phased sequence — cash first, spending discipline second, capacity third, build last
  • Bridge stack — off-the-shelf tools running each function today, under $150/month total
  • Build gate — the custom platform is unlocked only by three consecutive net-positive months
  • Risk register — named risks with owners and mitigations, not disclaimers
Use case
The moment the project would normally dieA struggling business decides to fix itself by building software, spends six months and its reserve, and ends up with a half-finished app and the same cash problem. The gate makes that impossible: until the business is provably profitable, this document is a target architecture running on rented rails.
Depends on
OP-01 Gate tracker04 Build sequence05 Risk register
Verdict This is the most mature thinking in the entire concept and it is unusual to find it inside the product it constrains. "Buy before you build, and let profit fund the build" is the single sentence that makes the rest of the blueprint credible rather than aspirational.
03

Platform layer

Applies across all modules

Features that aren't a screen. These matter more than they look: the accessibility and language layer is the difference between software the owner uses and software the crew uses.

CapabilityWhat it doesWhy it belongs in a field business
Four languages
EN · ES · HT · FR-CA		Runtime translation of navigation, section titles and descriptions, with English as the fallback dictionary.English, Spanish and Haitian Creole are the working languages of South and Central Florida field crews. A dispatch tool nobody on the truck can read is an owner's tool, not a company's.

Dark · Light · High contrast		Full token swap including chart restyling; preference persists between visits.Light mode exists for one reason: a phone screen in Florida sun at 1 PM. High contrast serves low-vision users and the same glare problem.

04

Cross-module loops

Where the value actually lives

Individually, most of these modules have off-the-shelf equivalents. The argument for one system is that three loops close automatically instead of depending on someone remembering to connect them.

LoopPathWhat closing it is worth
Cash loop Job done → invoice → reminders 7/14/30 → payment → bucket funded → threshold met → Overview clears Turns collections from a task someone remembers into a state the system reports. $1,570 outstanding is the first leak, and it's visible before it's overdue.
Paper loop Receipt photographed → transcribed → reviewed if uncertain → PDF → Drive + DB + mirror → auto-tagged → statement → export One capture produces a deductible, a searchable record, a backup and a line in a statement. The alternative is the same document handled four times, or not at all.
Lead loop Ad or missed call → auto text-back → quote form → booked with deposit → job → review request → rating up → cost per lead down Marketing that pays for itself twice: once in the booked job, once in the review that makes the next lead cheaper.
Discipline loop True cost from buckets → margin audit → reprice → syncs to quote form → next job priced correctly The only loop that fixes the root cause. Underpricing is usually invisible until the cost model is attached to the price book.
05

Build sequence

Six months to the gate

The order is deliberate and worth defending: revenue before discipline, discipline before capacity, capacity before code. Each phase funds the next.

Months 1–2 · active

Cash up, near-zero cost

Collections reminders, deposits on new work, tap-to-pay, the margin audit, missed-call text-back, and the review engine. Every item here raises revenue or recovers it, and none requires building anything. This is where the money to do the rest comes from.

Months 3–4

Spending controlled

Bucket thresholds enforced weekly rather than admired monthly. The statement rhythm locks in. Any service line that cannot cover its allocation is repriced or retired — the audit becomes a habit instead of an event.

Months 5–6

Capacity and scale

Partner exchange covers peak season without permanent payroll. Ad spend increases only from margin, never from reserve. Growth is allowed to be slower than demand.

The gate

Build BDash — after three consecutive net-positive months

Until then the bridge stack runs every function for under $150 a month and this document stays a specification. When the gate opens, build in loop order: paper loop first, because it feeds everything else, then cash, then lead.

Risk register

Named, owned, mitigated

Carried forward from the source concept and extended. The prototype already names most of these, which is unusual and to its credit — the additions below are the ones a build would hit first.

RiskExposureMitigation
ScopeAccounting, OCR, telephony, payroll, CRM and ad management are six separate industries with six sets of incumbents.The build gate, plus a standing rule: integrate where a mature product exists, build only the connective tissue.
Payroll, tax & worker classificationWithholding errors and crew rent/swap arrangements carry IRS and labor-law penalties that fall on the business, not the software.Accountant and attorney review before any transfer executes. The system tracks contracts; it does not determine status.
Single point of failureIf the dashboard is down, mail, phone, invoicing and payroll are down with it — on a day crews are already dispatched.Triple store, checkpoint restore, and bridge tools that remain usable standalone. Restores tested, not assumed.
Data concentrationClient contact details, employee records and financial data in one store raises the consequence of any single breach.Encryption at rest, per-role access, tested restores, and a real answer for the local mirror on portable hardware.

AdoptionSoftware changes nothing if the crew keeps the old habits. This is the most common cause of death for tools like this.One non-negotiable habit — the weekly 20-minute review — plus multilingual UI so adoption isn't gated on English.

07

Honest read

What to keep, cut, and defer

A blueprint that only praises the design isn't a blueprint. Summary judgement across all thirteen modules:

KEEP — BUILD FIRST

The spine

Digi-Scan → triple store → buckets → Overview. This chain is the product. It is coherent, defensible, and nothing else in the system works without it. The human-in-the-loop gate on uncertain transcriptions is a better decision than most commercial OCR products make.

Alongside it: collections, the margin audit and missed-call text-back — the three features that raise cash without requiring the platform to exist yet.

CUT OR INTEGRATE

The rebuilt wheels

The in-house mail client and the ad campaign manager. Both duplicate mature products, both are large builds, and neither is where this system's advantage lies. Sync mail threads onto the client file; read ad numbers in through an API. The value was always the attachment to the record, not the interface.

DEFER — NEEDS A PROFESSIONAL

The liability surface

Payroll processing, tax determination and crew-transfer contracts. The concept correctly gates these behind attorney and accountant review. Hold that line: track and export, don't calculate and file. The partner exchange board should generate contracts for review, never execute them.

THE REAL FINDING

The plan outranks the platform

The most valuable artifact in this concept isn't the dashboard — it's the Recovery Plan and the build gate attached to it. A struggling business that ran phase one and never wrote a line of code would be better off than one that built all thirteen modules first.

That the concept says so about itself, inside itself, is the reason it deserves to be built at all.

Standing note

All figures in the source concept are simulated. Payroll, tax, worker-classification and contract structures require a qualified accountant and attorney before any implementation touches a real business.
