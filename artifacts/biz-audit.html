Audit Protocol Diagnostic · Field Manual Rev. 01

Find the weak point before it finds you.

Most businesses do not fail from a cause nobody could see. They fail from a number nobody divided. This is the working procedure: what to pull, what to divide, what to notice without any documents at all, and how to turn all of it into an audit somebody can actually act on.

01

Pull the evidence

Ask for documents by what they betray, not by their name. Every request below has a reason attached — if you can't say what a document would reveal, don't ask for it.

Rank your evidence before you trust it

Tier 1 · HardestBank and merchant-processor statements. Money that actually moved.
Tier 2Filed tax returns and audited statements. Someone signed under penalty.
Tier 3Accounting software exports. Accurate only where reconciled — check the last reconciliation date.
Tier 4Management reports and internal dashboards. Built by the person being measured.
Tier 5 · SoftestRecollection. "We usually do about…" is a hypothesis, never a finding.

Rule of triangulation: any number that enters your audit must be provable two independent ways. Revenue from the P&L should reconcile to deposits. Labor hours from payroll should reconcile to the schedule. Where the two disagree, the gap is the finding.

The request list

One more request, always: the list of everything they refused to send. Reluctance is data. Note it, date it, and put it in the limitations section rather than pretending you saw it.

02

The denominators

A weakness hides by being reported as a total. Totals go up while the business gets worse. Divide by the thing that's actually scarce — hours, capacity, clients, days of cash — and the weakness has nowhere to sit. Fill in what you know; leave the rest blank.

Vital signsAwaiting inputs

Denominator traps

  • Growth without decomposition. Revenue up 12% means nothing until you split it into price, volume, and mix. Price-driven growth and volume-driven growth demand opposite responses.
  • Margin measured against revenue instead of capacity. A 40% margin on a job is irrelevant if the job consumed a crew-day you could have sold twice. Margin per unit of the scarce resource is the only version that ranks work correctly.
  • Averages over bimodal data. An average job value of $800 usually means a pile of $300 jobs and a few $4,000 ones. Always look at the distribution; the average client frequently does not exist.
  • Profit measured with unpaid labor. Owner and salaried weekend hours are a real cost of goods being donated. Price the donation at market rate and re-run the margin before you call anything profitable.
  • Counting churn but not shrinkage. Customers rarely quit. They quietly buy less. Cancellations are visible; a 30% spend decline across a third of the base is not, and it's bigger.
  • Trailing twelve months on a seasonal business. Annual figures smooth away the two months that actually decide the year. Look monthly, and look at the same month last year.
03

Tells, no documents required

These cost nothing to check and appear in no report. Half of them will produce your top finding before the financials arrive.

04

Build the thesis

A list of problems is not an audit. The thesis is one sentence naming the binding constraint — the single thing that, if unchanged, makes every other fix cosmetic.

Shape of a thesis sentence

This business is not revenue-constrained, it is quote-latency-constrained: 38% of inbound requests wait more than 48 hours for a price, and those close at 11% against 34% for same-day quotes. Roughly $214k of annual gross profit sits inside that delay, and no capacity investment will reach it.

Three parts, always: what people think the constraint is, what it actually is with the number attached, and what the wrong diagnosis would have cost.

Sort findings into four buckets

  • Ruin risks. Uninsured exposure, worker misclassification, expired license, single unbacked system, one person who can end the business by leaving. Fixed first regardless of return — you cannot compound from zero.
  • Leaks. Cash already earned and not collected or not charged. Aged receivables, unbilled work, price drift, discount without approval. Fastest payback in the audit.
  • Constraints. The ceiling. Whatever limits throughput once the leaks are sealed. Usually latency, scheduling, or a bottleneck person.
  • Noise. Real but immaterial. Write it down, say it's immaterial, drop it. Volume of findings is not quality of audit.

Rank what survives

Score each remaining finding as (annual $ impact × confidence) ÷ (weeks of effort × disruption), then check the ordering against reality: sequence matters more than score. Fixing pricing before fixing job costing means pricing off numbers you already know are wrong.

State how you could be wrong

For every finding, write the sentence that would falsify it. "If the callback log is incomplete and true rework is under 3%, this finding collapses." An audit that cannot be wrong is a sales document, and the person reading it can feel the difference.

Findings register

Compose findings here in the discipline above, then export the whole thing — ledger values included — as a markdown draft. Nothing is stored after you close the page, so export before you leave.

05

The audit itself

Structure it so a tired owner reading only the first page still knows what to do on Monday.

i

Scope and period

What you examined, what period it covers, who you spoke to, what you were not given access to. One paragraph.

ii

Thesis — one page, no exceptions

The constraint sentence, the three numbers that prove it, and the single decision you're asking for. If it spills to a second page, you haven't found the constraint yet.

iii

Method and evidence tiers

Which figures came from bank records, which from management reports. This is where your credibility is either established or quietly lost.

iv

Findings register

Grouped ruin / leak / constraint. Each one: claim, evidence, mechanism, annual cost, fix, owner, date, leading indicator, falsifier. Same shape every time so it can be scanned.

v

Remediation sequence — 30 / 60 / 90

Sequenced by dependency, not by size. Name one owner per item; two owners is zero owners. Include what you are explicitly choosing not to do this quarter.

vi

Instrumentation

The handful of numbers that must be computed weekly from now on, and where each one comes from. An audit that doesn't leave behind a measurement habit gets to be repeated in a year.

vii

Limitations

What you could not verify, what you assumed, what would change your conclusions. Write it before anyone asks.

viii

Reassessment date

A fixed calendar date to re-run the ledger against the same denominators. Findings without a re-check date become opinions within a month.
